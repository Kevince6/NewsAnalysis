L74: ## Overview
L75: 
L76: At the end of the second quarter of 2026, the general government gross debt to GDP ratio in the euro area (EA21) stood at 88.8%, an increase compared with 88.4% at the end of the first quarter of 2026. In the EU, the ratio also increased from 82.5% to 82.9%.
L77: 
L78: Compared with the second quarter of 2025, the government debt to GDP ratio increased in both the euro area (from 87.3% to 88.8%) and the EU (from 81.4% to 82.9%).
L79: At the end of the second quarter of 2026, the general government debt was made up of 84.5% debt securities in the euro area and 83.9% in the EU, 13.1% loans in the euro area and 13.8% in the EU and 2.4% currency and deposits in both the euro area and the EU.
L80: Due to the involvement of EU Member States' governments in lending to certain Member States, quarterly data on intergovernmental lending (IGL) are also published. The IGL as percentage of GDP at the end of the second quarter of 2026 stood at 1.3% in the euro area and at 1.1% in the EU.
L81: 
L82: These data are released by Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union.

L110: ## Government debt at the end of the second quarter of 2026 by Member State
L111: 
L112: The highest ratios of government debt to GDP at the end of the second quarter of 2026 were recorded in Greece (140.8%), Italy (139.9%), France (119.1%), Belgium (110.2%) and Spain (101.4%), and the lowest ratios were recorded in Estonia (26.1%), Denmark (27.1%), Luxembourg (28.2%) and Bulgaria (28.7%).
L113: 
L114: 【4†Image: General government gross debt to GDP ratio, 2026Q2】
L115: Compared with the first quarter of 2026, fifteen Member States registered an increase in their debt to GDP ratio at the end of the second quarter of 2026, ten registered a decrease, and the ratio remained stable in the Netherlands and Spain. The largest increases in the ratio were observed in Poland (+3.0 percentage points – pp), Portugal (+2.1 pp), Latvia (+1.9 pp), Lithuania (+1.6 pp), France (+1.4 pp), Italy and Malta (both +1.3 pp) and Estonia (+1.1 pp).
L116: The largest decreases were recorded in Cyprus (-3.0 pp), Greece (-2.7 pp), Hungary (-2.0 pp), Ireland (-1.8 pp), Croatia (-1.6 pp) and Slovakia (-1.0 pp).
L117: 【5†Image: Changes in general government gross debt to GDP ratio, 2026Q2 compared to 2026Q1】
L118: Compared with the second quarter of 2025, eighteen Member States registered an increase in their debt to GDP ratio at the end of the second quarter of 2026 and nine Member States registered a decrease. The largest increases in the ratio were recorded in Poland (+6.7 pp), Lithuania (+4.9 pp), Belgium (+3.8 pp), France (+3.7 pp), Luxembourg (+3.6 pp), and Estonia (+3.0 pp). The largest decreases were observed in Greece (-11.2 pp), Cyprus (-9.5 pp), Slovenia (-4.5 pp) and Portugal (-3.4 pp).

L74: ## Overview
L75: 
L76: In the euro area (EA20) the government deficit to GDP ratio remained unchanged at 3.0% in 2024 and 2025. In the EU, it also remained unchanged at 3.1%, the same level as in 2024. In the euro area the government debt to GDP ratio increased from 86.6% at the end of 2024 to 87.7% at the end of 2025, and in the EU from 80.3% to 81.5%.
L77: In this release, Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union, is providing government deficit and debt data for the years 2022-2025, based on figures reported by EU Member States in the second notification in 2026, for the application of the excessive deficit procedure (EDP). This notification is based on the ESA 2010 system of national accounts. This release also includes data on government expenditure and revenue.

L107: In 2025, all Member States, except Cyprus (+3.6%), Denmark (+2.9%), Greece (+2.0%), Ireland (+1.8%) and Portugal (+0.7%), reported a deficit. L110: ## Reservations on reported data
L111: 
L112: Eurostat is expressing a reservation on the quality of data reported by Bulgaria for 2025. Eurostat is discussing with the Bulgarian statistical authorities the appropriate national accounts recording of some operations of the Road Infrastructure Agency (RIA) ( Агенция Пътна инфраструктура). This will require additional data from the national authorities. Bulgaria is currently recording these amounts as impacting the 2025 deficit by 0.6% of GDP.
