US says it intercepted Iranian drones after Iran claims strike in Bahrain

The U.S. military said Friday it shot down two Iranian attack drones headed toward Bahrain, denying Iran's claim that it had struck a U.S. radar system in the kingdom. CENTCOM, the U.S. command center covering the region, said there were no injuries or damage.
Iran's official IRNA news agency cited a Revolutionary Guard statement that said the attack on the radar system followed a U.S. strike on a telecommunications pole in the southern Iranian port of Sirik. It was Iran's first report of targeting a facility in Bahrain since September. 
During an air war that began in February, Iran targeted U.S. bases in neighboring Arab nations including Bahrain in retaliation for U.S. strikes. Since then, Iran has routinely targeted oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway for international oil shipments.
Bahrain's Ministry of Information did not immediately respond to requests for comment. 

Iran says a tanker struck a mine in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said Friday that a liquefied petroleum gas tanker struck a mine, causing an explosion, while trying to sail through the Strait of Hormuz, according to the state-run IRNA news agency.
The Guard said the vessel ignored repeated warnings while transiting the eastern part of the strait. The claim could not be independently verified, and there was no immediate report of the incident from the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations, a British military agency that monitors incidents at sea.

Turkey and Qatar reach a mutual defense agreement

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad al-Thani signed a mutual defense agreement Friday in Istanbul, according to Erdogan's office.
In a statement posted on X, the office said the two sides would continue working together to prevent the resurgence of war in the Gulf region and "persuade the parties to resolve their problems through diplomacy."
There were no further details on the defense agreement between the countries, which already share deep military ties.
The deal follows a joint defense agreement signed between Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia in August in which the three nations pledged to treat an attack on one as an attack on all.

Iranians mourn 350 unidentified soldiers from Iran-Iraq war

More than 38 years after the Iran-Iraq war ended, the remains of hundreds of unidentified soldiers were carried through Iranian cities Friday in one of the largest such funerals in recent years.
In Tehran, thousands joined a procession for the remains of 110 soldiers. Funerals for another 240 were held in other cities. The remains were recently recovered from former battlefields.
Trucks carrying flag-draped coffins passed near Tehran University as mourners dressed in black crowded around them, many weeping. President Masoud Pezeshkian and senior officials attended the event.
