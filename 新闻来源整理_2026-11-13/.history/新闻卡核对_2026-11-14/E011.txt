L13: ## Key Highlights
L14:   * As hundreds of Palestinians begin returning to their homes in two neighbourhoods of Hebron city following a 10-day operation by Israeli forces, humanitarian organizations are supporting local authorities in assessing damage and addressing outstanding needs.
L15:   * Settler violence and access restrictions continue to disrupt the olive harvest, with 377 olive-harvest-related settler attacks recorded since the start of the season in September, already exceeding the total recorded during any previous harvest season; farmers continue to face physical assaults and theft of harvested olives and equipment.
L16:   * Heavy rainfall and flash floods damaged shelters, belongings and livestock fodder of 37 Palestinian households across seven Bedouin communities in the northern Jordan Valley.
L17:   * In the Gaza Strip, strikes continue to cause casualties in populated areas and affect humanitarian operations; attacks have caused reported civilian casualties and hit the vicinities of humanitarian convoys and facilities.
L18:   * The Food Security Sector warns that the daily production and free distribution of meals could reduce by approximately 45 per cent if the current Israeli suspension of the entry of humanitarian and commercial supplies from Egypt continues.
L19:   * As winter approaches, Israeli restrictions on the entry of engine oil and spare parts threaten essential water, sanitation and health services. Shelter conditions remain abysmal, with families’ needs far exceeding the limited resources available to the Shelter Cluster.
L20:   * Maternal and newborn health services are overstretched, with neonatal unit occupancy reaching 151 per cent and special-care baby unit occupancy 199 per cent in September.

L29: The humanitarian situation across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, continued to be affected by Israeli forces' operations, settler violence and restrictions on Palestinian movement and access to essential services. During the reporting period, 58 Palestinians, including two children, were injured: 43 by Israeli settlers and 15 by Israeli forces.
L30: Additionally, two Israeli solidarity activists were physically assaulted and injured by Israeli settlers on 1 November in Tell al Khashaba, near Aqraba (Nablus). Of the Palestinian injuries, 49 (84 per cent) were in the context of settler attacks, including 44 linked to the olive harvest. A total of 64 settler attacks resulting in casualties or property damage were recorded.
L31: In Hebron city, following a shooting attack by two Palestinians on 23 October that killed two Israeli soldiers and injured four others, an operation by Israeli forces between 23 October and 2 November reportedly displaced approximately 3,000 Palestinians from two neighbourhoods, although displacement figures remain under verification (see below).
L32: In Khirbet Suruj, near Al Yamun in Jenin governorate, seven Palestinian families comprising 33 people were displaced on 29 October after Israeli authorities reportedly ordered them to leave, claiming the area as state land and threatening to demolish their structures.
L33: Access to specialized health care remained constrained by permit requirements. According to the World Health Organization (【2†WHO†www.emro.who.int】), 76 per cent of the 93,051 patient permit applications submitted to Israeli authorities between January and October 2026 to access medical treatment in East Jerusalem or Israel were approved, while the remainder were denied or remained pending by the time of the scheduled appointment.
L34: Although this represents an increase from 64 per cent in 2025, the approval rate remained below pre-October 2023 levels.
L35: Access to education continued to be disrupted by Israeli forces' operations, movement restrictions and settler violence, particularly in Masafer Yatta, Hebron governorate. On 1 November, Israeli forces detained 10 teachers from Jinba Basic School for approximately five hours while they were returning home.
L36: The incident compounded longstanding 【3†restrictions】 on school transportation and access to education in the area, including the confiscation of vehicles and restrictions on coordinated access to communities within “Firing Zone 918.”
L37: During the reporting period, Israeli authorities demolished or forced Palestinians to demolish 31 Palestinian-owned structures, displacing 71 people, including 38 children, and otherwise affecting 72 people. Of the total, 24 structures were demolished for lack of Israeli-issued building permits and seven on punitive grounds. The punitive demolitions belonged to families of Palestinians killed or arrested for carrying out attacks against Israelis.
L38: ### Humanitarian impact of the operation by Israeli forces in Hebron city
L39: 
L40: KEY FIGURES  |
L41: --- | ---

L43: between 23 October and 2 November according to Hebron Municipality; displacement figures remain under verification  | serving approximately 4,450 students remained inaccessible on 1-2 November
L44: On 2 November, Israeli forces ended a 10-day operation in Hebron city that began on 23 October, during which extensive movement restrictions were imposed and over 3,000 Palestinians were reportedly displaced from the Jildah and ‘Ein Deir Bahah neighbourhoods. Following the operation, displaced families began returning to their homes, with reports of damage to household belongings, including in homes reportedly used as military positions by Israeli forces.
L45: The extent of damage, displacement and outstanding humanitarian needs remain under assessment.
L46: On 30 October, Israeli forces carried out punitive demolitions of two inhabited residential apartments in ‘Ein Deir Bahah, belonging to the families of two Palestinians who killed two soldiers in the 23 October shooting 【4†attack】 and were killed in an exchange of fire with Israeli forces. The demolitions, carried out using explosives, caused severe damage to adjacent apartments, including windows, doors and water tanks.
L47: Three families comprising 15 people, including nine children, were displaced, while neighbouring families were otherwise affected. According to affected families, Israeli forces also arrested eight relatives of the attackers, including one woman, and physically assaulted the father of one of the attackers, causing injuries requiring medical treatment.
L48: During the preceding week, approximately 90,000 students under the Hebron Directorate of Education were affected by the temporary suspension of in-person learning, with schools operating through assignments. While in-person learning began resuming on 1 November, 11 schools serving approximately 4,450 students and employing 246 education personnel remained inaccessible on 1 and 2 November due to movement restrictions imposed during the operation.
L49: Following the withdrawal of Israeli forces, inspections of affected schools were undertaken to check for hazardous remnants and ensure the safety of students and staff ahead of the planned resumption of in-person learning on 3 November. Psychosocial support is also being provided to affected families and children attending the 11 schools.
L50: On 2 November, members of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) visited Hebron city, meeting with local authorities and humanitarian organizations and visiting affected neighbourhoods to assess the humanitarian impact of the operation and outstanding needs. Local authorities and humanitarian partners continued coordinating the response to affected families, including the provision of one round of emergency multi-purpose cash assistance, with a second round under consideration based on assessed needs.
L51: As displaced families began returning, priority needs under assessment included emergency shelter repairs, non-food items and assistance to families whose homes and belongings were damaged.
L52: ### Settler violence and olive harvest season
L53: 
L54: KEY FIGURES  |
L55: --- | ---
L56: 64  | 377
L57: Harvest-related settler attacks in one week (27 October - 2 November)  | Harvest-related settler attacks since the season began (1 September – 2 November)
L58: Settler violence remained at high levels, with 64 attacks resulting in casualties or property damage recorded across 48 communities during the reporting period, compared with a weekly average of 53 such attacks so far in 2026. October recorded the highest monthly number of such attacks since the UN began documenting these incidents in 2006, with 457 recorded by 31 October.
L59: During the reporting period, Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian homes in several communities in Hebron governorate, particularly in Masafer Yatta. In Khirbet al Majaz, settlers physically assaulted residents and damaged their home and belongings, while homes and property were damaged in Mughayir al Abeed and Halaweh.
L60: In Nablus, settlers obstructed municipal road rehabilitation works, after which Israeli forces detained and physically assaulted two municipal workers, preventing the completion of repairs.
L61: The olive harvest continued to be disrupted by settler violence and restrictions on farmers’ access to agricultural land. In Hebron and Bethlehem governorates, Israeli authorities suspended previously scheduled access near settlements during the Hebron city operation, subsequently indicating that arrangements would resume subject to daily confirmation. Even where access was coordinated or permitted, farmers continued to face restrictions and settler attacks.
L62: In Beit Iksa (Jerusalem), settlers and Israeli forces reportedly forced farmers to leave their olive groves despite “prior coordination,” while farmers reported that settlers had already harvested olives from approximately 30 trees. In Deir Ammar (Ramallah), access was limited to three days and 15 farmers aged over 40; an 82-year-old farmer was subsequently assaulted by settlers while harvesting during the scheduled period.
L63: Between 27 October and 2 November, 41 olive-harvest-related settler attacks resulting in casualties or property damage were documented, including eight in Areas A and B. In Jamma’in and Qabalan (Nablus), settlers assaulted farmers harvesting in Area B, including two men over 70 years old. In Kafr Malik (Ramallah), settlers accompanied by Israeli forces reportedly attacked farmers, injuring a person providing protective presence and forcing farmers to leave.
L64: In the area between Al Masu’diya and Burqa (Nablus), settlers reportedly assaulted a farmer harvesting in Area A and seized six bags of olives.
L65: Humanitarian partners continued supporting farmers through harvesting tools and machinery, volunteer harvesting campaigns, protective presence and accompaniment, and Cash for Protection assistance. However, settler violence and access restrictions continued to constrain the response, with assistance reaching only a limited number of farmers in most locations.

L72: On 27 October, heavy rainfall and flash floods damaged residential and livestock shelters, household belongings and livestock fodder, affecting 37 Palestinian households across seven Bedouin communities in the northern Jordan Valley, in Tubas governorate, according to a rapid needs assessment by humanitarian partners.
L73: Shelter Cluster partners provided 195 bedding kits, 37 kitchen kits and five emergency tents, while WASH partners responded with 37 hygiene kits, two mobile latrines and two water storage tanks. Additional flooding impacts were reported in Aqbet Jaber Camp, other parts of Jericho governorate and refugee camps across the West Bank, where technical assessments were ongoing to inform potential longer-term WASH interventions.
L74: On 29 October, the Israeli Jerusalem District Court issued an interim order prohibiting Israeli authorities from obstructing the repair and reconstruction of tents, residential shelters and livestock structures damaged or destroyed by the floods in Khirbet Mak-hul on 27 October. The order allows the restoration of affected structures in their original locations and dimensions.
L75: The flooding further exposed the vulnerability of Palestinian communities in the Jordan Valley, where demolitions, settler violence and restrictions on construction and repairs compound displacement risks and hinder recovery.
L76: ### Humanitarian response activities at a glance – West Bank
L77: 
L78: 27 October - 2 November 2026 (unless otherwise specified)
L79: 
L80: Shelter: Partners reached 504 people (98 households), primarily through shelter repairs, non-food items and cash assistance in Hebron, Nablus and Ramallah governorates. In Hebron, partners supported local authorities in addressing emergency shelter needs following the Israeli forces’ operation, mainly through the distribution of blankets, mattresses and other essential household items.
L81: Food Security: In Hebron city, partners provided approximately 500 food parcels for distribution by municipal teams following the Israeli operation. Elsewhere, partners provided agricultural livelihood support for the olive harvest, including harvesting tools and collective harvesting.
L82: Water, Sanitation and Hygiene: In Hebron city, WASH Cluster partners distributed hundreds of family hygiene and menstrual hygiene kits, along with bottled drinking water, to families affected by the Israeli forces’ operation. In the northern Jordan Valley, partners provided 37 hygiene kits, two mobile latrines and two water storage tanks to flood-affected households across seven communities.
L83: In Masafer Yatta (Hebron), emergency water trucking remained suspended in four communities, affecting 975 people, while deliveries to six other communities continued at reduced frequency due to movement restrictions and settler attacks.
L84: Health: As of October, 872 health service delivery points were reported as fully or partially functional (382 fully and 490 partially), while 22 were non-functional. Mobile health services continued in Area C, H2 and displacement-affected areas, with 227 mobile clinic locations supported by 13 mobile teams and 11 health partners.
L85: In Hebron governorate, as of 3 November, mobile clinic services remained suspended in 10 locations, while improved access enabled partners to resume services in several previously inaccessible areas. In response to the Israeli forces’ operation, one partner delivered emergency medical supplies to five hospitals and 25,992 units of normal saline to six hospitals in the southern West Bank, alongside 612 psychosocial support kits for children and 368 dignity kits for women affected by the operation.
L86: Child Protection: Partners' puppet shows reached 2,020 children, while awareness activities on emergency preparedness and explosive hazards reached 2,840 students. In Nablus and surrounding communities, movement restrictions constrained service delivery, with partners conducting remote case management follow-up for 25 children and registering 14 new cases.
L87: Since the beginning of 2026, partners have provided case management and referral services to 1,613 children, including 533 children with disabilities, and cash-for-protection assistance to 372 children and caregivers across the West Bank.
L88: Education: Following the Israeli forces’ operation in Hebron city, affected schools were inspected for hazardous remnants ahead of the planned resumption of in-person learning on 3 November.
L89: ## Gaza Strip
L90: 
L91: The reporting period is 27 October to 2 November 2026 unless otherwise specified.
L92: Israeli airstrikes, shooting and shelling continue to hit populated areas across the Gaza Strip, causing casualties, including among displaced people. On 3 November, small arms fire reportedly hit near an UN-contracted facility in Deir al Balah and a UN-organized convoy in Rafah.
L93: On the same day, gunfire hit and damaged multiple tents in a displacement site in northern Rafah, reportedly causing injuries and forcing an international non-governmental organization to leave the area, where it was working on flood control ahead of the winter rainy season.
L94: According to the Ministry of Health in Gaza, which operates under the de-facto authorities, 36 Palestinians were reported killed and 145 injured between 27 October and 2 November, bringing the total number of reported casualties since the ceasefire agreement to 1,593 fatalities and 5,748 injuries. Another 18 fatalities were retroactively added to the total number after their identification details were approved by MoH.
L95: According to the Logistics Cluster, 466 UN and partner trucks carrying humanitarian supplies were manifested for entry into Gaza between 26 October and 1 November. Of these, 397 were offloaded on the Israeli side of Kerem Shalom – 195 from Ashdod, 127 from Egypt and 28 from Jordan. Compared with the previous week, the number of trucks manifested fell by 28 per cent, while the proportion offloaded truckloads out of all those manifested declined by 5 per cent.
L96: Overall, partners have been progressively reporting declining humanitarian cargo available to ship due to funding constraints.
L97: The resumption of commercial truck entry through Kerem Shalom after a temporary suspension reported in mid-October brought basic commodity prices back to early-October levels. The private sector collected 498 commercial truckloads into the Gaza Strip between 26 October and 1 November, according to Gaza Chamber of Commerce data shared with the Cash Working Group. Only six commercial trucks carried non-essential items, just over one per cent of the total.
L98: On the evening of 1 November, Israeli authorities notified UN agencies that humanitarian and commercial shipments from Egypt would be suspended, with some exceptions, citing smuggling concerns. Humanitarian partners have raised concerns that if the suspension remains in place, prices and access to essential supplies could be affected.
L99: In particular, the Food Security Sector warns that the daily production and free distribution of cooked meals could reduce from approximately 600,000 to 330,000 and of bread from 160 to 139 metric tons as a result.
L100: ### Shortages of engine oil threaten critical services
L101: Shortages of engine oil and lubricants, spare parts and maintenance supplies for generators, engines and vehicles threaten service delivery across Gaza. The Israeli authorities continue to impose particularly tight restrictions on the entry of these and other critical items, including new generators. Additionally, all services depend heavily on regular, predictable fuel supplies, which require sustained funding until more sustainable power solutions can be implemented.
L102: These solutions depend on Israeli authorities granting permission to bring in solar panels, reconnect the electricity grid and restart the Gaza Power Plant.
L103: Following are a few examples of services threatened by these shortages:
L104:   * Water and sanitation: Systems for pumping floodwater and sewage, particularly critical during the upcoming rainy season, are already failing. On 1 November, the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility 【5†warned†www.facebook.com】 of cascading failures in Gaza’s water and sanitation systems.
L105:   * Health: Critical life-support systems as adult and neonatal ventilators could cease functioning, diagnostic and clinical services could be disrupted, cold-chain capacity compromised and ambulance services and other essential health transport interrupted.
L106:   * Sanitation: Trash collection capacity is already severely below solid waste production levels, as only 50 per cent of vehicles used to manage waste remain operational; pressures on the remaining vehicles are high. During the reporting period, a Palestinian was killed when sand collapsed on him as he dug a cesspit by hand in Gaza city because modular cesspit systems and materials needed to connect latrines to the nearest sewage network are largely unavailable.
L107: Critical supply gaps undermine preparations for the cold and rainy season
L108: 
L109: The Shelter Cluster estimates that, as of 1 November, people in Gaza still needed approximately 121,000 tents, 561,000 tarpaulins, 57,000 emergency shelter kits (ESKs) and 1.7 million mattresses ahead of the cold and rainy season, alongside tens of thousands of other essential shelter and household items.
L110: However, the Shelter Cluster’s ability to respond is hampered by severely depleted stocks in Gaza, which currently comprise approximately 2,800 tents, 76,000 tarpaulins, 4,993 mattresses, while no ESKs are available as the entry of timber continues being rejected by the Israeli authorities. Other essential items as winter blankets or toolkits are also in extremely short supply compared to the vastity of needs.
L111: Items procured but awaiting entry outside Gaza are equally limited, including approximately 2,000 tents, 71,000 tarpaulins, 3,100 ESKs and no mattresses.
L112: Most items in the pipeline are still under ongoing or planned procurement; reported partner pipelines include approximately 59,000 tents, 703,000 tarpaulins, 13,000 ESKs and 33,000 mattresses, alongside other critical items.
L113: However, due to a combination of lack of, or limited funding, lengthy procurement processes, restrictions on entry and delayed approvals by the Israeli authorities, these supplies may not be procured and shipped until the second half of winter, making the next two months critical for meeting urgent winterization needs.
L114: Even accounting for items currently under procurement and in the pipeline, a substantial funding gap of US$186.5 million remains against the total estimated winterization requirements of US$267.9 million, further compounded by ongoing challenges in securing approvals and facilitating the timely entry of shelter materials into Gaza.

L9: ## Key Highlights
L10:   * Heavy rains hit the Gaza Strip from the evening of 6 November 2026 through the weekend, causing widespread flooding, the collapse of damaged buildings, damage to shelters, humanitarian facilities and learning spaces, and disruptions to essential services. An infant reportedly drowned and several people were reportedly killed when a sand dune collapsed on their tents.
L11:   * Humanitarian partners mobilized immediately to assess people’s needs, distribute emergency supplies and otherwise support those affected. Nevertheless, humanitarian stocks of tents, tarpaulins, blankets and clothes inside Gaza are virtually exhausted.
L12:   * By 8 November, humanitarian partners received appeals from over 26,000 households whose shelters or other belongings were damaged, with the overall impact believed to be greater. Partners have assessed the needs of 1,877 households, and provided support to 422 households, including with non-food items, at 29 displacement sites.
L13:   * Over 150,000 tents purchased by humanitarian partners and Member States are outside Gaza ready to be dispatched. This includes about 117,000 tents from the Egyptian Relief Committee alone in Al-Arish.
L14:   * Humanitarian funding and Israeli approvals are urgently needed so that enough supplies and machinery can be brought into Gaza, including vacuum trucks, mobile dewatering pumps, engine oil, hoses and heavy machinery.
L15: ## Overview
L16: Heavy rain and strong winds hit the Gaza Strip from 6 November through the weekend, causing floods affecting thousands of people. It damaged displacement sites, shelters and humanitarian facilities, and disrupted services across all five governorates. Most Palestinians in Gaza remain displaced, and an estimated 65 per cent live in tents or makeshift shelters – that is 1.4 million people.
L17: Following the start of the storm, humanitarian partners reported being overwhelmed by the high number of appeals for support.
L18: According to the Site Management Cluster (【2†SMC†reliefweb.int】), since the beginning of the heavy rain until midnight 7 November, about 268 weather-related alerts had been recorded affecting over 26,000 households. An estimated 600 households are thought to have been displaced once again due to the storm. According to SMC, over 6,000 family shelters have reportedly been destroyed and 17,400 otherwise damaged. Additionally, 66 latrines have reportedly been destroyed and 618 damaged.
L19: SMC reported that over 3,900 households are reported to have no shelters currently. Site Management partners report that more than 20,000 households across 222 sites, severely affected by the heavy rain, need shelter and non-food items support (tarpaulins, shelter repair materials, bedding, tents and clothing).
L20: Beyond that, 26 sites need latrine rehabilitation, 32 need drainage and engineering support, 37 need lighting support and 156 need sandbagging support, and more than 10,000 households report needing cash assistance. These figures are preliminary and are subject to assessments and verification.
L21: Mine Action partners highlighted the potential risks associated with flooding, as explosive ordnance may become exposed, buried under surface or debris, or displaced to previously unaffected areas. This creates additional dangers for civilians and humanitarian personnel, including those involved in debris removal.
L22: The floods exacerbated existing structural and environmental risks, including unsafe buildings and unstable sandy areas with at least 4,000 high-risk buildings identified by partners so far. Three partial or full building collapses were reported, with no casualties. An infant reportedly drowned at a displacement site.
L23: At Al Berka Reservoir, west of Deir Al Balah, a sandy hill collapsed onto tents, killing five displaced people, including three children and two women, and injuring one, according to Civil Defense who responded to the incidents.
L24: The Civil Defense reported on 7 November that dozens of displacement sites, tents, and even entire residential areas, were flooded, with water levels reaching 1.5 metres in some locations. Its emergency rooms received 2,166 distress calls, including from families whose homes were already damaged by hostilities. Rescue teams, working with municipalities and others, were able to respond to 218 calls, but given the volume of the calls, most callers were only provided with guidance.
L25: The Civil Defense calls for the entry of winter rescue equipment, machinery, plastic sheeting, tents, and prefabricated mobile shelters for displaced people. The Gaza Municipality 【3†received†www.facebook.com】 251 incident notifications and 84 distress calls on 6 and 7 November, covering rainwater pooling and rising water levels in streets and displacement sites.
L26: It cited major challenges, including extensive damage to stormwater drainage networks and infrastructure, a shortage of pumping equipment and heavy machinery, and difficult access to some sites.
L27: According to the WASH Cluster, shortages of vacuum trucks, mobile dewatering pumps, engine oil and hoses continue to limit the WASH response capacity. At Sheikh Radwan Lagoon, heavy water inflows are overwhelming pumping capacity, while stormwater mixed with sewage from damaged networks poses public health risks.
L28: According to the Education Cluster, heavy rain and flooding affected at least 55 learning spaces serving about 55,000 children. Twenty of the learning spaces were damaged, with learning activities suspended. At the other affected spaces, attendance has fallen significantly, to as low as 3 per cent. Poor light and power supply challenges add to the disruption. Moreover, seven service points addressing gender-based violence were destroyed due to the heavy rain.
L29: One partner reported 200 affected families across two sites for persons with disabilities. Families with disabilities, women and girls, and unaccompanied children face heightened risk.
L30: Due to the heavy rain, on 7 November, at least eight community kitchens temporarily cut production by 50 per cent (about 25,000 fewer meals). On 8 November, production returned to normal, with maintenance and assessments continuing at some. A kitchen in Gaza city producing about 1,000 meals daily closed on 7 November because of disrupted power supply and difficulty lighting cooking fires; it resumed operations on 8 November.
L31: Flooding, waterlogging and damage to already fragile roads delayed meal deliveries and food stock dispatch to kitchens, and some locations were hard to reach, putting partner staff at risk. The weather also worsened the condition of a partner’s vehicles, that already faced shortages of engine oil and spare parts, so 7,250 planned meals could not be delivered to 10 shelters on 7 November; deliveries were back to normal on 8 November, and other delayed distributions were completed as conditions improved.
L32: Some partners’ warehouses were affected, and agricultural land rehabilitation through cash-for-work was suspended because workers could not reach the sites.
L33: Health facilities also suffered leaks and flooding. UK-Med facilities and Nasser Medical Complex had water entering several departments, and the basement of Patient’s Friends Benevolent Society Hospital housing the pediatric department, pharmacy and radiology, flooded. Two clinics (An Nakheel and Al Yasmin) were damaged by flooding and wind, and two medical points (At Tuffah and Bir 19) lost tents and roofing. Services were also disrupted.
L34: One Health Cluster partner suspended all sexual and reproductive health services, fixed and mobile, and another suspended its mobile clinics. A third partner suspended its seven mobile teams for a day before resuming. Médecins du Monde recorded a 54 per cent drop in consultations, as flooded roads delayed staff movement. Partners report more cold exposure, weather-related illness and psychosocial distress, and expect higher caseloads once attendance recovers.
L35: Existing medicine shortages (antipyretics, antibiotics, antiemetics, antidiarrheals) are worsening. Surveillance is being strengthened for acute watery diarrhea, respiratory infections, skin infections and acute jaundice syndrome.
L36: The humanitarian community had spent months preparing for the rainy season, knowing that living conditions in Gaza leave hundreds of thousands of people highly vulnerable to severe weather. Many people are living in worn-out tents and makeshift shelters that provide little protection from heavy rain and strong winds, including many in low-lying areas prone to flooding. Others remain in damaged buildings at risk of collapse.
L37: As part of the preparedness efforts, more than 150,000 tents, 300,000 tarpaulins and 500,000 blankets were outside Gaza ready for dispatch. However, humanitarian efforts to prepare were constrained by Israeli restrictions on the entry of supplies and equipment and funding shortfalls, limiting the stocks available inside Gaza when the rains began.
L38: Israeli restrictions on Palestinian access to large parts of Gaza also leave people with fewer options to voluntarily relocate to safer areas less prone to flooding.
L39: As of 8 November 2026, humanitarian partners need – as a matter of urgent priority – more Israeli approvals to bring into Gaza critical supplies including shelter items, engine oil, spare parts, and heavy machinery, and more funding.
L40: ## Humanitarian response activities at a glance
L41: 
L42: 7-8 November 2026 (unless otherwise specified)
L43: 
L44: Shelter: Partners distributed 12 tents, 1,864 mats, 474 mattresses, and 1,714 humanitarian-standard tarpaulins.
L45: Site Management: Partners installed 50,000 sandbags to prevent displacement sites from being flooded; overall since September, they have installed more than 135,000 sandbags. Desludging of cesspits is under way by partners at displacement sites in Al-Mawasi (Khan Younis), with 30 per cent of the targeted sites completed.
L46: They also distributed winter clothes to 3,364 households, including 8,432 children, across 19 sites in Khan Younis and Deir Al Balah, and blankets to 896 households across six sites in Gaza city and North Gaza.
L47: Debris Working Group: Partners used heavy machinery to support flooded shelter and displacement sites, responding to appeals from municipalities and partners. About 40 sites were reached on 7 November, and by 8 November the number of affected sites had grown to 200, with 30 sites supported by the UN, and 21 by bilateral committees. By 7 November, a bilateral committee had completed 18 emergency sand works at sites to improve ground conditions and reduce flood risk.
L48: Before the storm, debris partners had strengthened about 130 vulnerable sites through clearance, ground levelling, drainage improvements and access route stabilization, using over 100,000 tons of debris.
L49: Health: A partner provided tarpaulins, clothes and one tent to 16 affected people, and another partner distributed tents and rain protection materials to 298 households and winter clothes to 2,100 people. A third partner mobilized emergency contractors and deployed backup vehicles so staff could reach all six primary health care centres, kept primary health care services running and delivered 849 consultations on 7 November.
L50: A fourth partner kept all 16 of its medical points and health centres open during the rains, and its mobile teams have resumed following a suspension due to the heavy rain.
L51: Water, Sanitation and Hygiene: Partners are dewatering affected sites. Coastal Municipalities Water Utility crews are operating 15 dewatering pumps to remove rainwater from tents and have provided municipalities with mobile pumping units and additional vacuum trucks. About 25,000 sandbags were allocated to reinforce sites and protect shelters.
L52: UN partners cleared solid waste from stormwater inlets and drainage canals, unblocked sewage lines and stormwater drains, and used suction trucks to remove accumulated water mixed with sewage in 58 shelters. A cluster partner deployed machinery to 20 sites and distributed hygiene kits, while others provided sand, sandbags and essential repair tools, and cleared sediment and debris from 10 critical locations and rehabilitated roads at four sites using crushed sand and gravel.
L53: Protection: One partner provided tarpaulins, clothed and one tent to 16 families in Nuseirat (Deir al Balah). Teams assessed and responded to the needs of at least 350 families with persons with disabilities at an inclusive site in Az Zawaida (Deir Al Balah). One partner helped families voluntarily relocate away from the shoreline, and another began shelter and sanitation repairs at Hayat displacement site.
L54: A partner working with persons with disabilities reported 200 affected families across two camps, and 66 families in Az Zawaida received emergency shelter kits. Before the storm, partners managing shelters for vulnerable groups had reinforced tents, improved drainage, raised ground levels and distributed winter clothing. At a site for women with cancer in Gaza city (65 households), 57 new tents, additional tarpaulins and 1,409 winter clothing items were distributed.
L55: Addressing Gender-Based Violence: Three partners distributed their remaining stocks of tarpaulins, clothing, and tents. One partner distributed 40 tents. Another distributed 764 dignity kits over two days across Gaza and Deir Al Balah governorate. A third provided tents to families in Deir Al Balah and Gaza city and assisted three beneficiaries in North Gaza. A UN agency and another partner delivered 1,200 menstrual hygiene management kits and 200 dignity kits to the Joint Response Mechanism.
L56: One partner opened an emergency safe space for women and girls at its office to keep mental health and psychosocial support and case management going, and the Jabalya safe space was repaired after wind damage and reopened.
