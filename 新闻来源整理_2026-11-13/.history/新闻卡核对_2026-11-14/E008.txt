L33@P2: I. Overview of the Drug Enforcement Administration
L34@P2: Introduction
L35@P2: Since its establishment through an Executive Order in July 1973 to create a single, unified
L36@P2: command to combat “an all- out global war on the drug menace,” the Drug Enforcement
L37@P2: Administration (DEA) has evolved from a small, domestic-oriented law enforcement agency to a
L38@P2: globally recognized organization. The DEA works to ensure the safety and health of American
L39@P2: communities by combating criminal drug networks bringing harm, violence, overdoses, and
L40@P2: poisonings to the United States. Accordingly, the DEA is advancing its core “North Star”
L41@P2: mission priorities—strengthening enforcement; dismantling the Mexican cartels; disrupting
L42@P2: chemical supply chains; targeting illicit finance; leading the fight on the cyber and dark web
L43@P2: frontlines; building strategic domestic and foreign partnerships; reducing violence and protecting
L44@P2: communities; and prioritizing employee wellness. To accomplish this mission, approximately
L45@P2: 10,000 personnel—Special Agents, Diversion Investigators, Intelligence Research Specialists,
L46@P2: Forensic Chemists, and professional staff, are assigned to over 300 DEA offices across the globe.
L47@P2: The DEA brings to the criminal and civil justice system of the United States, or any other
L48@P2: competent jurisdiction, those organizations and principal members of organizations, involved in
L49@P2: the growing, manufacture, or distribution of controlled substances appearing in or destined for
L50@P2: illicit traffic in the United States; and to recommend and support non-enforcement programs
L51@P2: aimed at reducing the availability of illicit controlled substances on the domestic and
L52@P2: international markets.
L53@P2: Since 2021, synthetic opioids have claimed
L54@P2: nearly 325,000 American lives; however,
L55@P2: there has been a recent downward trend in
L56@P2: overdose deaths for the first time in years. In
L57@P2: May 2025, the Centers for Disease Control
L58@P2: and Prevention announced that drug overdose
L59@P2: deaths had fallen 27%, from roughly 110,000
L60@P2: in 2023 to about 80,000 in 20241
L61@P2: . Provisional
L62@P2: data through late 2025 projects a further drop
L63@P2: to around 72,000. The DEA’s lab testing now
L64@P2: shows 29 percent of fentanyl pills analyzed
L65@P2: during Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 contained a
L66@P2: potentially lethal dose – a dramatic reduction
L67@P2: from the 76 percent of pills tested just two years ago.
L68@P2: Fentanyl powder purity has also dropped to 10.3
L69@P2: percent, down from 19.5 percent during the same
L70@P2: period. These reductions in potency and purity correlate with a decline in synthetic opioid deaths
L71@P2: to levels not seen since April 2020.

L73@P2: Concentration of Jalisco New Generation Cartel and
L74@P2-3: Sinaloa Cartel Affiliates in the U.S. (October 2024)3
L75@P3: Calendar Year (CY) 2025 was a pivotal year for the DEA marked by record-breaking
L76@P3: enforcement actions and significant policy shifts. In total, during 2025, the DEA seized more
L77@P3: than 9,800 pounds of fentanyl and 47.2 million fentanyl pills, removing an estimated 369 million
L78@P3: potentially deadly doses of fentanyl2 from communities across the U.S. The DEA also seized
L79@P3: over 78,000 kilograms of methamphetamine, 4.2 million methamphetamine pills, over 260,000
L80@P3: kilograms of cocaine, nearly 2,000 kilograms of heroin, over 3,000 guns, and over $600 million
L81@P3: in total assets.
L82@P3: Notably, in May 2025, the Department of Justice announced the “largest fentanyl bust in DEA
L83@P3: history,” revealing that a DEA-led operation had dismantled one of the most prolific fentanyl
L84@P3: trafficking organizations in the U.S. In coordinated pre-dawn raids across five states (New
L85@P3: Mexico, Oregon, Utah, Arizona, and Nevada), agents arrested 16 suspects linked to the Sinaloa
L86@P3: Cartel (Cartel de Sinaloa) and seized an unprecedented cache of drugs and weapons. In
L87@P3: Albuquerque, New Mexico alone, officers seized
L88@P3: approximately 3 million fentanyl pills and 11.5 kilograms of
L89@P3: fentanyl powder. This “record-shattering” takedown was a
L90@P3: significant blow to the Sinaloa Cartel and a major victory in
L91@P3: protecting Americans from the fentanyl scourge.
L92@P3: Recent U.S. Government policy shifts have allowed the DEA
L93@P3: to aggressively target every part of the Mexican cartels’ global
L94@P3: supply chain, including the thousands of individuals across the
L95@P3: United States who work for the cartels and distribute fentanyl
L96@P3: over social media and in communities. The Trump
L97@P3: Administration has adopted an increasingly aggressive stance
L98@P3: against major cartels, exemplified by the early-2025
L99@P3: designation of specific cartels, including the Sinaloa Cartel
L100@P3: and Jalisco New Generation Cartel (Cartel de Jalisco Nueva
L101@P3: Generacion or CJNG), as Foreign Terrorist Organizations
L102@P3: (FTOs). The DEA has identified tens of thousands of Sinaloa
L103@P3: Cartel members, associates, and facilitators operating
L104@P3: worldwide – in at least 40 countries. Jalisco New Generation
L105@P3: Cartel also operates globally in at least 40 countries.
L106@P3: In Mexico, the cartels produce fentanyl and methamphetamine in clandestine laboratories. Most
L107@P3: fentanyl is pressed into pills resembling legitimate medication and sold to customers in the U.S.
L108@P3: through social media platforms and messaging applications. To relentlessly attack the Mexican
L109@P3: cartels specifically, the DEA is developing a strategic approach that applies simultaneous,
L110@P3: threat-based, data and intelligence-driven pressure across cartel leadership, logistics, finance,
L111@P3: and infrastructure. Investigations and operations are focused to leverage cross-regional
L112@P3: enforcement actions that are sequenced and synchronized across U.S. agencies and with
L113@P3: international partners to prevent cartels from reforming and reconstitution, while enabling
L114@P3: Mexican partners to sustain long-term pressure within Mexico.
